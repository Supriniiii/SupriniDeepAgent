# Purpose

The Service-Oriented Architecture (SOA) organizes capabilities into coarse-grained, reusable services that expose business functions through standardized interfaces. Services are orchestrated through an enterprise service bus (ESB) that handles routing, transformation, and protocol mediation, enabling integration of heterogeneous systems across the enterprise.

---

# Objectives

- Expose business capabilities as reusable, discoverable services
- Enable integration of heterogeneous legacy and modern systems
- Provide centralized orchestration and mediation through the service bus
- Support protocol and data format transformation between systems
- Enable service reuse across multiple business processes
- Provide governance over service lifecycle and contracts
- Support gradual modernization of legacy systems through service wrapping

---

# Scope

This architecture covers enterprise service orientation with service bus, service registry, orchestration, and mediation capabilities. It does not cover individual service internals, microservices decomposition, or direct point-to-point integration without mediation.

# Design Principles

- Service reusability: Services designed for reuse across multiple consumers
- Standardized contracts: All services expose well-defined, versioned interfaces
- Loose coupling via ESB: Services communicate indirectly through the service bus
- Protocol agnosticism: ESB mediates between different protocols and formats
- Composability: Business processes composed from existing services
- Discoverability: All services registered and discoverable through the registry

# Scalability Strategy

SOA scales through the ESB layer which can be clustered for high throughput. Individual services scale independently behind the ESB. The bus provides load balancing across service instances and can throttle consumers to protect backend services. Service registry caching reduces discovery overhead under high load.

---

# Reliability Strategy

Reliability is achieved through the ESB's built-in retry, timeout, and fallback mechanisms, redundant ESB nodes for high availability, and service virtualization that enables transparent failover. Transaction coordination through the orchestration engine provides consistency for multi-service processes.

---

# Security Considerations

- ESB handles authentication and authorization centrally for all services
- Protocol-level security (WS-Security, OAuth) applied at the mediation layer
- Service contracts include security requirements enforced at the bus
- Audit logging centralized at the ESB for all service interactions

# Deployment Considerations

- ESB deployed as clustered middleware with dedicated infrastructure
- Services deployed independently but registered in the central registry
- Legacy adapter deployment coordinated with legacy system maintenance windows
- Contract versioning managed through the governance layer
- ESB configuration changes require change management approval

---

# Validation Requirements

- All services must be registered with valid contracts before being routable
- Protocol transformations must preserve semantic meaning of data
- Orchestrated processes must maintain transactional consistency
- Service SLAs must be monitored and enforced by the ESB
- Legacy adapters must correctly translate to and from modern interfaces
